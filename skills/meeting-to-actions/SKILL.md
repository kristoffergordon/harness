# Meeting to actions

Turn raw notes or a transcript into something people act on. Stay faithful to what was said, and be honest about what is unclear.

## Rules

- **Only what was said.** Do not add decisions, owners or dates that nobody stated. If something is implied but not confirmed, put it under open questions, not under decisions.
- **Separate the kinds of content.** A decision is something agreed. An action is a task someone took on. An idea or opinion is neither. Mixing them is the most common failure.
- **Resolve relative dates** ("next Friday", "end of the month") against the meeting date. If the meeting date is unknown, ask or leave them relative and flag it.
- **Names and terms** stay as spoken. If the same person or system appears under two names, flag it rather than guess. A transcript in another language gets output in English unless asked otherwise.
- **Sensitive content** such as personnel remarks, commercial figures or off-the-record comments goes only into the internal notes, never into a message for external readers. Say that you left something out and why.

## Output

### Summary
Two or three sentences: what the meeting was for and what came out of it.

### Decisions
One line each, with who made or agreed it and the reasoning if it was given.

### Actions
A table: **action (starts with a verb), owner, due date, source**. Where the owner or date was not stated, write `unassigned` or `no date` and list the item again under open questions. Do not fill the gap.

### Open questions and risks
Things raised but not settled, disagreements that were left standing, and dependencies on people who were not in the room.

### Follow-up message
A short message to the attendees: what was decided, who does what by when, and what needs confirming. Plain and brief, in the tone of the meeting. Write it as a draft for the user to send, and leave it unsent.

## Check

- Every action has a verb, an owner and a date, or is marked as missing one.
- No action appears without a source in the notes.
- Nothing in the follow-up message goes beyond what the attendees heard.

Finish by listing the three things most worth confirming with the attendees before anything is sent.
